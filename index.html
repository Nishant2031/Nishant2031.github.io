About

I grew up in Nepal, the land of Mount Everest and the birthplace of Gautam Buddha. Then I moved to Florida for college, which is about as big a change in weather as you can get. Somewhere along the way, I became an engineer who spends most of his time thinking about one thing: heat.

I studied Mechanical Engineering at the University of South Florida, with minors in Aerospace and Physics, and right after graduating I joined Prof. Patrick Hopkins' ExSiTE Lab at the University of Virginia, where I'm now a PhD student. Show me a hot chip, a battery pack, or a packed server rack, and my first question is where the heat is going and how to get it out faster. That's just how thermal engineers are: we don't really like heat. Sometimes I think that's the real reason I left Florida.

Right now, heat is one of the biggest things holding computing back. Chips keep getting more powerful, and every extra watt has to go somewhere. That's the problem I want to spend my career on. In the lab, I study how heat crosses the interfaces between materials, layers only nanometers thick, using lasers that track temperature changes over a few trillionths of a second, all the way down to cryogenic temperatures.

Coming to Virginia was also about wanting a change and seeing more of the world. I'm a huge football fan (yes, the one people here call soccer), and a Messi fan through and through. Following the game has taught me more about cities around the world than any geography class, so I always have a list of places I want to visit. Norway is high on it, but as a science person, my go-to would be Germany.

Outside the lab, I also follow the stock and options markets. I'm not a finance guy, but I can't resist a good pattern, so I keep my own spreadsheets and try to figure out what's moving and why. I'm also the person who has to try every new piece of tech, and nothing gets me more excited than a new way to cool something down. When my head gets too full, I go for a long walk and let things settle.

If you want to talk heat transfer, cooling, or football, I'm always up for it.

Research

EXSITE LAB · UVA · 2026–PRESENT

Non-equilibrium heat transport at interfaces

In modern chips, much of the thermal resistance hides in layers only nanometers thick: between the die, the thermal interface material, and the package. I use pump-probe thermoreflectance to measure thermal conductivity and thermal boundary conductance in thin films, and to study how phonons and electron–phonon coupling govern heat flow when carriers and the lattice fall out of equilibrium.
